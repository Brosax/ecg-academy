# Research: haemodynamics

> Written 2026-10-16 for the schematic blood-flow layer (血流示意, `src/view3d/flowControls.ts`, `core/flowTiming.ts`). Numbers carry citations. Textbook values are rounded consensus figures (chapter numbers refer to the editions named); the model constants they are compared with are named with their source file. Items under "Design choices" are not physiology and must be treated as tunable. There is no `.json` twin yet: no test reads these numbers directly.

## Summary

FILLING: once the AV valves open, most ventricular filling is passive (rapid filling, driven by relaxation and recoil), then diastasis, then the atrial kick. The kick is about 20 % of filling at rest (Guyton), about 10 % at rest and slow rates but up to about 40 % at fast rates (Klabunde); its share grows with age and with stiff ventricles (ASE/EACVI 2016). Model: the reference beat at 75/min gets about 0.76 of its filling by the end of rapid filling, 0.80 by the end of diastasis and 0.20 from the kick; beat by beat the shares come from the volume model of §6 (a fixed 14 mL kick, so its share grows with rate); `src/sim/measure/mechanics.ts`, `src/ecg/wiggers.ts`.
TIMING: IVCT 34-36 ms, ejection 281-293 ms, IVRT 92-94 ms (TDI, see activation-physiology.md §5). LVET ≈ 413 − 1.7·HR ms and QS2 ≈ 546 − 2.1·HR ms in men (Weissler 1968), ≈ 285 / 388 ms at 75/min. About 70 % of the stroke volume leaves in the first third of ejection (Guyton). Model: aortic opening at QRS onset + 65 ms; aortic closure at T end + 10 ms but never later than QRS onset + QS2(HR of the preceding RR), so ejection shortens with rate and after a short RR; IVRT shortens 2 % per 10/min; IVCT stays 35 ms (§6).
VOLUMES: EDV ≈ 120 mL, ESV ≈ 50 mL, SV ≈ 70 mL, EF ≈ 60 % (Guyton); ASE/EACVI 2015 men 106 ± 22 / 41 ± 10 mL, EF 62 ± 5 %. Model: 120 / 50 / 70 mL for the reference beat; beat to beat SV(filling time, kick, RR₁, RR₂) (§6): AF beats vary, a beat with no filling does not eject (pulse deficit), the beat after a PVC is potentiated, complete heart block fills to ≈ 137 mL (SV ≈ 87 mL).
OXYGEN: arterial SaO2 ≈ 97-98 % (PO2 ≈ 95-100 mmHg); mixed venous SvO2 ≈ 75 % (PO2 ≈ 40 mmHg); coronary-sinus blood is the most desaturated in the body. Red = O2-rich and blue = O2-poor is a drawing convention: deoxygenated blood is dark red, and veins look blue through skin because of how tissue scatters and absorbs light (Kienle 1996).
TRANSIT: only part of the LV end-diastolic volume enters and leaves in the same beat ("direct flow", roughly a third to under a half in 4D-flow MRI); the rest stays for one or more beats (Bolger 2007, Eriksson 2010). With EF ≈ 0.6 the mean residence is EDV/SV ≈ 1.7 beats.

## 1. Passive filling and the atrial kick  _(confidence: high for the ordering, medium for the exact shares)_

When ventricular pressure falls below atrial pressure the AV valves open and blood that pooled in the atria during systole rushes in: the rapid-filling phase, driven by ventricular relaxation and elastic recoil, not by the atria. Flow then nearly stops (diastasis) until atrial systole adds the last part. Guyton & Hall give about 80 % of filling before atrial contraction and about 20 % from it, describing the atria as primer pumps. Klabunde gives about 10 % at rest and at low heart rates, rising to as much as 40 % at high rates, when diastasis vanishes and early and late filling run together. Doppler mitral inflow shows the same split as the E wave (early, passive) and the A wave (atrial). In healthy young adults E/A is above 1; with age, hypertrophy or impaired relaxation E/A falls and the atrial share rises (ASE/EACVI 2016). Losing the kick, as in atrial fibrillation, matters most in those patients.

Model and layer: the filling profile (`fillProfile` in `wiggers.ts`, shared with the flow layer) has rapid filling over R = clamp(0.5·(fill end − mitral opening), 40, 160) ms, slow diastasis over the rest of the window outside the kicks, and a smoothstep kick per atrial contraction that lands while the AV valve is open. Its shares come from the volume model (§6): diastasis carries 0.02 mL/ms, the kick a fixed 14 mL, rapid filling the rest. For the reference beat that is 0.76 / 0.04 / 0.20, close to the textbook 75 % / 80 % / 20 % (`WIGGERS.diastasisFrac`, `FILL_FRACTIONS`); with no kick (AF) rapid filling carries about 95 %. So the passive E surge is visibly the dominant inflow and the kick a small top-up, as the textbooks say. Between the shared knots the two curves differ in shape: the Wiggers volume is a monotone cubic whose slope is zero at aortic and mitral opening, while the flow advance (`ejectedFrac`, rapid filling 1 − (1 − s)²) is fastest there. The largest gaps are about 0.11 of the stroke volume in early ejection and 0.23 in mid rapid filling (`tests/view3d/flowTiming.test.ts` prints them), a known deviation from the 0.03 / 0.05 agreement the flow plan aimed for.

## 2. Isovolumic phases and ejection  _(confidence: high)_

The repo already cites tissue-Doppler normal values (Biering-Sørensen 2016, n = 974): IVCT 34-36 ms, ejection time 281-293 ms, IVRT 92-94 ms (activation-physiology.md §5; `MECH` in `src/sim/measure/truth.ts` uses IVCT 35 and IVRT 93 ms). During both isovolumic phases all four valves are shut and no blood crosses any valve, which is why the layer freezes every stream then. Weissler's systolic time intervals give LVET ≈ 413 − 1.7·HR ms (men; 418 − 1.6·HR in women) and PEP ≈ 131 − 0.4·HR ms, i.e. about 285 ms and 100 ms at 75/min. Ejection is front-loaded: about 70 % of the stroke volume leaves in the first third of ejection, the rest in the last two thirds (Guyton). The model's profile `ejectedFrac(u) = 1 − (1 − u)^2.2` gives 59 % at u = 1/3, slightly less front-loaded.

Known model offset: aortic opening = QRS onset + 30 + 35 ms and, at 75/min, aortic closure = T end + 10 ms, so ejection lasts QT − 55 ms, about 300 ms on the normal heart (QT 354 ms), a little longer than the 281-293 ms above. The flow layer inherits this from cycle truth on purpose (one clock for leaflets, Wiggers and flow); the scenario test logs it. At faster rates the QS2 cap of §6 takes over.

Sources:
- Biering-Sørensen T et al. Cardiac time intervals by tissue Doppler imaging M-mode: normal values… PLoS One 2016;11:e0153636 (full reference in activation-physiology.md §5).
- Weissler AM, Harris WS, Schoenfeld CD. Systolic time intervals in heart failure in man. Circulation 1968;37:149-159.
- Guyton and Hall, 14th ed., ch. 9 (period of ejection).

## 3. Volumes  _(confidence: high)_

Textbook adult values: EDV about 110-120 mL, SV about 70 mL, ESV about 40-50 mL, EF about 60 % (Guyton & Hall; Klabunde). Echocardiographic normal ranges (ASE/EACVI 2015, biplane): men EDV 106 ± 22 mL, ESV 41 ± 10 mL, EF 62 ± 5 %; women 76 ± 15 mL, 28 ± 7 mL, 64 ± 5 % (lead-geometry-anatomy.md §6a). Right and left stroke volumes are equal on average; only the pressures differ. The model's reference beat (`WIGGERS`, `VOLUME`: EDV 120, ESV 50 mL, EF 58 %) is a rounded textbook choice; §6 makes the volumes beat to beat.

Sources:
- Lang RM et al. Recommendations for cardiac chamber quantification by echocardiography in adults: an update from the ASE and EACVI. J Am Soc Echocardiogr 2015;28:1-39.e14. doi:10.1016/j.echo.2014.10.003
- Guyton and Hall, 14th ed., ch. 9 (end-diastolic volume, stroke volume, ejection fraction).

## 4. Oxygen saturation and the red / blue convention  _(confidence: high)_

Arterial blood leaves the lungs with PO2 about 95-100 mmHg and haemoglobin about 97-98 % saturated; after the tissues have taken their share, mixed venous blood in the pulmonary artery has PO2 about 40 mmHg and SvO2 about 75 % (Guyton & Hall ch. 41; Boron & Boulpaep ch. 29). Myocardium extracts more oxygen than most tissues, so coronary-sinus blood is far more desaturated (around 30 %; Klabunde). Blood colour changes only in the lungs and in the tissues, never inside a normal heart: the right heart and pulmonary artery carry O2-poor blood, the pulmonary veins and left heart O2-rich blood ("artery" and "vein" name the direction relative to the heart, not the oxygen content).

Venous blood is not blue. Deoxyhaemoglobin is darker red; superficial veins look bluish through skin because skin and tissue scatter and absorb red and blue light differently at depth (Kienle 1996). "Venous blood is blue" is a classic misconception among students (Michael 2002). The layer therefore says, in the legend, that red and violet-blue are a convention and gives SaO2 ≈ 98 % and SvO2 ≈ 75 %.

## 5. Transit through the ventricle  _(confidence: medium; the four fractions are approximate)_

4D-flow MRI pathline studies split the LV end-diastolic volume into four parts: direct flow (enters and is ejected in the same beat), retained inflow (enters but stays), delayed ejection (was already there and leaves) and residual volume (stays for at least two beats). In healthy volunteers direct flow is the largest part but well under the whole, roughly a third to under a half of EDV, and residual volume is about a quarter to a third (Bolger 2007; Eriksson 2010; check the papers before quoting exact percentages). A simpler bound gives the same picture: with EF ≈ 0.6 only SV/EDV ≈ 60 % of the ventricle's content leaves per beat, so a blood parcel stays on average about EDV/SV ≈ 1.7 beats. "Each parcel goes through the heart in one beat" is therefore wrong.

Sources:
- Bolger AF, Heiberg E, Karlsson M et al. Transit of blood flow through the human left ventricle mapped by cardiovascular magnetic resonance. J Cardiovasc Magn Reson 2007;9:741-747.
- Eriksson J, Carlhäll CJ, Dyverfeldt P et al. Semi-automatic quantification of 4D left ventricular blood flow. J Cardiovasc Magn Reson 2010;12:9.

## 6. Beat-to-beat mechanics: rate, RR, atrial timing  _(confidence: high for the directions, medium for the rate laws, low for the model's magnitudes)_

**Mechanical intervals and heart rate.** In Weissler's systolic time intervals the whole electromechanical systole QS2 (QRS onset to aortic closure, A2) shortens by about 2.1 ms per beat/min (546 − 2.1·HR in men), almost all of it in ejection (LVET 413 − 1.7·HR), while the pre-ejection period changes little (131 − 0.4·HR); with atropine-driven rate changes in normal men LVET and QS2 regress on heart rate but PEP does not (Kelman et al. 1981). Normally QT is a little shorter than QS2 (QT − QS2 = −26 ± 13 ms) and parallels it across resting rates (Boudoulas 1982); the "electromechanical window" QAoC − QT is +22 ± 19 ms in controls and negative in long-QT syndrome (−43 ± 46 ms; ter Bekke 2015), i.e. mechanical systole does not lengthen with a long QT. Relaxation hardly depends on rate: the time constant of isovolumic pressure fall fell only from 52.6 to 48.2 ms between 110 and 160/min in dogs (Weiss 1976). In AF, filling time depends on the RR interval far more than ejection time does; below an RR of about 400 ms filling largely moves into the next beat's pre-ejection period, and the short beat's own QA2 shortens so the mitral valve opens early; IVCT and IVRT did not explain the changes (Cieslinski 1984).

Model (`ventricularTimes` in `src/sim/measure/truth.ts`, HR = 60000 / RR before the beat): mitral closure at QRS onset + 30 ms; IVCT 35 ms at every rate (the PEP evidence above; a shorter IVCT would also leave the right-heart IVCT, 16 ms shorter in the model, below the 35 ms leaflet travel); aortic closure at min(T end + 10 ms, QRS onset + 546 − 2.1·HR), ejection ≥ 80 ms; IVRT 93 ms × (1 − 0.02 per 10/min above 75), 55-115 ms. At RR 800 ms every value equals the old constant, so the normal beat is unchanged. At 150/min A2 comes 231 ms after QRS onset, about 75 ms before T end: the model's QT follows pure APD restitution (Fridericia-like, no sympathetic shortening), so its QT − QS2 is positive at fast rates; this is a documented deviation from Boudoulas' resting data. The long-QT scenario gets a negative window at 75/min, as in ter Bekke. Result: sinus 150/min and VT at 150/min fill about 120 ms per beat, where the fixed intervals left none.

**Filling time and stroke volume.** In AF stroke volume tracks the preceding RR interval below about 600 ms and is nearly flat above it, the time needed to complete rapid filling, and at the same preceding interval a shorter pre-preceding interval gives a larger stroke volume (pulsed-Doppler study of 12 patients, doi:10.1007/BF01797720, abstract only). Beyond filling, contraction strength follows the preceding interval (mechanical restitution: a positive curvilinear relation once short pre-preceding intervals are excluded) and is potentiated after a short pre-preceding interval (Hardman 1998). After a premature beat the first sinus beat has a larger stroke volume and ejection fraction (post-extrasystolic potentiation, PESP); it is not explained by the Frank–Starling effect of the pause, since it persists without a compensatory pause and with controlled loading, and is attributed to calcium handling (Kuijer 1990; reviews citing Cooper 1993). A beat after a very short RR fills so little that its pressure pulse is too small to palpate: the AF pulse deficit (apical rate > radial rate). With a slow ventricular rate, as in complete heart block, the long diastole gives a large stroke volume (congenital complete block: "stroke volume large", normal output at rest), and when such patients are paced, stroke volume varies inversely with rate through EDV while ESV stays the same (J Clin Invest 1964 studies, abstracts only).

Model (`src/sim/measure/mechanics.ts`, read from cycle truth only, shared by the Wiggers panel and the flow layer): filling time T = this mitral closure − the previous mitral opening; filling F = V_r·(1 − e^(−T/90 ms)) + 0.02 mL/ms·T (+ 14 mL if a kick lands in the window), V_r fitted so the 75/min beat (T = 373 ms) gets exactly 70 mL. Contractility C = R(RR₁)/R(RR₂), R(RR) = 1 − 0.5·e^(−(RR − 200)/150) (C = 1 in any steady rhythm). EDV = max(ESV₀ + F, previous ESV), SV = C·(EDV − ESV₀) with ESV₀ = 50 mL, EF ≤ (EDV − 20)/EDV, EDV ≤ 160 mL. A beat whose QRS comes before the previous mitral opening (T < 2 ms) gets no filling: truth.ts gives it no semilunar events, so the leaflets stay shut, the flow shows no ejection and SV = 0 — the extreme of the pulse deficit (7 of about 23 beats in the 12 s AF trace; every such beat follows an RR < 450 ms). Results on the HRA heart: sinus 150/min SV ≈ 53 mL; AF 0-78 mL; PVC at 75/min (QRS 400 ms after the previous one, before its mitral opening at + 457 ms) does not eject — the beat after the compensatory pause ejects ≈ 93 mL (C = 1.15); PVC with the sinus at 60/min ejects ≈ 24 mL and is interpolated; complete heart block ≈ 87 mL. The magnitudes (V_r, τ, q_d, k, the 14 mL kick) are model choices fitted to the reference beat, not measurements. The Wiggers aortic pulse pressure is 40 mmHg × SV/70, and the aorta runs off exponentially in diastole (τ = 2.74 s, fitted to 95 → 80 mmHg over the 75/min diastole), so complete heart block shows a wide pulse pressure with a low diastolic pressure and a non-ejecting beat shows none.

**Atrial contraction against shut AV valves.** When atria and ventricles are dissociated (complete heart block, VT with AV dissociation, premature beats) some atrial contractions fall while the tricuspid and mitral valves are shut; atrial pressure then rises steeply and the jugular pulse shows a cannon a wave. They are irregular with AV dissociation and premature beats, regular when every P′ follows the QRS (junctional rhythm, isorhythmic dissociation) (StatPearls; Ranjith case report). In atrial flutter the atria keep contracting at the flutter rate (organised appendage flow every ≈ 200 ms); atrial function is reduced and recovers within weeks after ablation, and the atrial contribution can be restored by AV timing in paced patients (flutter Doppler studies, abstracts only). AF has no organised atrial contraction.

Model: the rhythms built from ventricular cycles (flutter, complete AV block, VT, junctional) now carry one atrial systole per A event (P onset + 50 ms, 110 ms or at most 0.55 of the atrial cycle) in `mech` only: they never become a P wave or `pOn`, so the ECG and the oracle tests see no change. `atrialEvents` classifies each contraction by its peak (onset + 45 %): a kick when the mitral valve is open, a cannon a wave when it is shut. Only kicks fill; a cannon gives a tall LA a wave in the Wiggers panel (20 mmHg, about twice the normal a wave: schematic) and the flow caption "cannon a wave". In the HRA scenarios junctional rhythm has a cannon every beat; complete heart block mixes kicks and cannons; the PVC's buried sinus P is a cannon; in VT the 75/min sinus P happens to stay locked to the 150/min VT (2:1), so every P lands in diastole and the stroke volume alternates with and without a kick.

**Cycle truth for unconducted ventricular beats.** A PVC or other ventricular beat that no P conducted gets its own cycle starting at its QRS onset. It takes over the non-conducted sinus P that falls in it (P onset within 300 ms before its QRS or during its QT), so the PVC, the buried P and the compensatory pause are one cycle, and the cycle before ends its TP at the PVC. This is a modelling choice (engine.ts `aCycles`): it keeps the NowStrip, timeline and Wiggers on the PVC's systole instead of switching to the blocked P halfway through it.

## Design choices (not physiology)

- **Half a path per beat.** Each stream has N dashes and advances an integer m ≈ N/2 dashes per valve window, so a comet travels about half its path per beat. This keeps the picture consistent with §5 (blood does not leave in one beat) while the integer m keeps the loop seamless when the window restarts.
- **Motion blur at fast surges.** At 1x playback the rapid-filling and early-ejection surges move a comet by more than half a dash per 60 Hz frame (up to about 1.5 dashes on the mitral stream at 75/min, about 4 in sinus tachycardia). A point-sampled dash pattern would then alias like a wagon wheel, so the comets would seem to freeze or run backwards, which would read as regurgitation. The shader therefore averages each comet over its travel since the previous frame. From half a dash per frame on, the average covers a whole dash, so the stream shows as an even bright streak until it slows down again (`cometBlur` in `src/view3d/bloodFlow.ts`). At slower playback the comets stay sharp.
- **Shape carries direction, colour carries the convention.** The bright head of each comet leads and the tail trails, so direction does not depend on colour vision. Colours (`FLOW_COLORS`): O2-rich #e0402c / #ff5a4a, O2-poor #5960b0 / #9b8fe8 (light / dark). Each is at least 3:1 against its scene background and kept away from QRS magenta, the vector blue, atrial amber and the coronary and cardiac-vein overlays (`tests/view3d/flowColors.test.ts`, which also checks deutan and protan simulations).
- **Intensity carries the volume.** The flow intensity q is the instantaneous volumetric flow (openness × dA/dt × the window's volume: the beat's SV, or the next beat's filling) over the reference beat's peak flow, so the 75/min beat peaks at 0.8 and bigger beats (complete heart block, post-PVC) up to 1, weak ones (AF after a short RR) less; the advance A still runs 0 → 1 per window so the dash loop stays seamless (`flowTiming.ts`).
- **Cannon caption.** A cannon a wave lasts about 110 ms; its caption has priority over the ventricular phase it interrupts and is held for at least 250 ms so the fixed-size chip does not flash for one 100 ms tick.
- **What is not drawn.** No backflow into the veins (pulmonary-vein Ar wave, the reflux of a cannon a wave: the cannon is captioned and drawn on the Wiggers LA trace only), no vortex, no velocity magnitudes, right and left stroke volumes taken as equal. These are listed in the About dialog (`about.limit.flow`).
